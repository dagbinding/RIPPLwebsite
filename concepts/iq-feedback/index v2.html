What RIPPLiQ can say.

RIPPLsense measures how the board moves. Everything about your body is the RIPPLiQ model's read of that motion, so the overlay always says which is which: numbers from the board are stated plainly, and the body is offered as the likely cause. The rule that keeps it watch-sized: one score, one cue, three numbers, one likely cause.

measured by RIPPLsense

The board's motion

  • Rail angleHow far the board leans into each turn (up to 46° on this wave).
  • Turn rateHow fast the board rotates through a turn, in degrees per second (about 200°/s in the bottom turns here).
  • Speed in and outWhat each turn costs or earns you, in km/h.
  • Weight transferThe board's pitch: nose up when your weight is back, nose down when it moves forward, and how far it swings after a turn.
  • Rail to railHow quickly you roll from one rail to the other, and how long you stay stuck on a rail before releasing.
  • Where on the waveRide height: up and down the face at each turn.

Putting it on the film.

  1. Film is the Higgsfield clip (app_watch_video, 1280 × 720, 12 s). The watch is tracked frame by frame, in position and size, so the brackets and tethers stay on it as the hand bobs and comes closer. The overlay runs on the film's clock and the film holds its last frame if the overlay runs longer.
  2. Start at is when the overlay begins, in film seconds. 2 s lands it while the watch still says "Fall detected", and the verdict's "Next set in 1:15" arrives just as the watch switches to its next-set screen at about 4.6 s.
  3. Photo and Your clip aren't tracked: use Move watch to drag the brackets onto the watch screen and set its size. It's remembered on this browser.
  4. To composite in an editor: Green screen (it keeps the film's timing and tracking), then Clean (or H) and Fullscreen, and screen-record at 1920 × 1080. Key out the green and lay it over the clip from frame 0.
  5. The ride is real: session 2272. Rail, turn rate, speed and pitch come from the board's log. The rider is rebuilt by our pose model and stood on RIPPL's board CAD; it's smoothed over about 0.1 s and played back on curves, since the model rebuilds the body independently at 10 fps. The wave score and wave number are placeholders.
