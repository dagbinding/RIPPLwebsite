What RIPPLiQ can say.

RIPPLsense measures how the board moves, and for now that's all the site talks about: every cue is something the board did, in numbers you can check. The RIPPLiQ model can also read your body from that motion; that stays off the site until we're ready to stand behind it. The rule that keeps it watch-sized: one score, one cue, one number.

The board's motion

  • Rail angleHow far the board leans into each turn (up to 46° on this wave).
  • Turn rateHow fast the board rotates through a turn, in degrees per second (about 200°/s in the bottom turns here).
  • Speed in and outWhat each turn costs or earns you, in km/h.
  • Weight transferThe board's pitch: nose up when your weight is back, nose down when it moves forward, and how far it swings after a turn.
  • Rail to railHow quickly you roll from one rail to the other, and how long you stay stuck on a rail before releasing.
  • Where on the waveRide height: up and down the face at each turn.

Putting it on the film.

  1. Film is the Fall video: the wipeout, underwater, then the cut to the POV watch shot. Landscape (1280 × 720, 17 s, cut at 5.5 s) on desktop and tablet; on phones, or with Vertical, the vertical edit (406 × 720, 10.9 s, cut at 5.8 s) with its own layout. The watch is tracked frame by frame in both, in position and size, so the brackets and the verdict's grow-out stay on it.
  2. Start at is when the overlay begins, in film seconds, kept per layout: 6.6 s landscape and 7.0 s vertical, as each watch comes up showing "Fall detected".
  3. Photo and Your clip aren't tracked: use Move watch to drag the brackets onto the watch screen and set its size. It's remembered on this browser.
  4. To composite in an editor: Green screen (it keeps the film's timing and tracking), then Clean (or H) and Fullscreen, and screen-record at 1920 × 1080. Key out the green and lay it over the clip from frame 0.
  5. Built for the page. The landscape film is 3.7 MB and the vertical 1.1 MB (re-encoded from 26 MB and 4.7 MB), and neither loads until the section is on screen; a 32 KB poster holds the frame until then, and the film pauses when you scroll away. The rider is one 20 KB still, drawn once (and again only if the card resizes). The animation loop runs only while the overlay is building or the film is playing on screen, then stops. Phones, tablets and touch screens get solid cards instead of frosted glass.
  6. The ride is real: session 2272. Rail, turn rate, speed and pitch come from the board's log. The rider is rebuilt by our pose model and stood on RIPPL's board CAD; it's smoothed over about 0.1 s and played back on curves, since the model rebuilds the body independently at 10 fps. The wave score and wave number are placeholders.
